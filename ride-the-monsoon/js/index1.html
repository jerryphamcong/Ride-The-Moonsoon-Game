Presentation companion

How this game meets the project

Networks & exchange

Indian Ocean ports link East Africa, Arabia, South Asia, and East Asia through monsoon sailing, high-value goods, and merchant communities.

Eight decisions

Departure, cargo, navigation, storm response, merchant selection, cultural exchange, protection, and return timing each have resource and story consequences.

Reasoning skills

Contextualization appears in the seasonal choices; causation appears in the storm and protection decisions; comparison and continuity/change frame ports and navigation tools.

Social & economic change

Trading activity rewards profitable but ethical choices, showing how ports grew as multilingual commercial communities while risk and unequal power shaped outcomes.

Innovation & culture

Lateen sails, the magnetic compass, port knowledge, and shared religious and language traditions appear as practical advantages, not trivia.

Historical notes

The setting uses broad, well-attested Indian Ocean patterns from 1200–1450: seasonal monsoons, Swahili coast commerce, Calicut's diverse port, and navigation technologies used across the sea.

Suggested research starting points: course materials, The Metropolitan Museum of Art Heilbrunn Timeline essays on Indian Ocean trade, and Oxford Reference entries on monsoon trade.

✦Historical context

Around 1325, merchants of the Swahili coast and the Malabar shore timed their crossings to the reversing monsoon. Crews read the sky, the stars, and a magnetic needle, and traded in ports where several languages, faiths, and currencies met.
